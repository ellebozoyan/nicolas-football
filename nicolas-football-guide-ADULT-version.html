Every line on the football field has a name and a job. The field is a giant map of yards — every 5 yards a stripe, every 10 yards a number. Once you know the map, the game makes sense.

The lines and what they mean

End zone The painted area at each end of the field, 10 yards deep. Get the ball into the OTHER team's end zone = TOUCHDOWN!"Where touchdowns happen."
Goal line The line at the front of each end zone. Cross it WITH the ball = touchdown."Cross this line with the ball."
Goal post The yellow "Y" at the back of each end zone. Kick the ball through the uprights = field goal (3 pts) or extra point (1 pt)."Kick through the Y."
50-yard line (midfield) The line in the middle of the field. Where most kickoffs happen."Middle of the field."
Yard lines The white lines every 5 yards. Big numbers every 10 yards tell you how far from the nearest end zone."Counts the yards left."
Sideline The long lines on each side. Step on or over it WITH the ball = OUT."Step on it = out."
Hash marks The little ticks inside the field. Where the ball is placed when it's spotted between plays."Ball goes between these."
Line of scrimmage An imaginary line at the spot of the ball each play. NO crossing it before the snap or it's a penalty."Don't cross till the snap."

Football grows with you

Football has stages. Start with flag football (no tackling), build skills, then maybe move to full pads. There is no rush. Every great player started where you are.

🏈

Catch Stage

What you learn here: how to hold the ball, how to throw a spiral, how to catch with two hands. Basics for the backyard. This is where every move becomes yours.

"Two hands. Look it in."
🎽

Flag Stage

What you learn here: flag football. 5-on-5 or 7-on-7. Pull flags instead of tackling. Real positions: quarterback, receiver, defender, rusher.

"Pull the flag."
🏃

Game Stage

What you learn here: running plays, route running, reading the defense. Real downs (you get 4 tries to go 10 yards). The game gets fast — so now your brain starts playing too.

"Now I think and play."
🏆

Player Stage

What you learn here: the full game. Real positions. Real strategy. Maybe full pads when you're older. By now your skills, your rules, and your spirit are all yours.

"My game. Forever."

Football has clear rules. Once you know them, every game makes sense. We'll focus on flag football here — that's the right form for now.
🎯

1. The object of the game

Move the ball into the OTHER team's end zone = TOUCHDOWN (6 points). Most points wins. That's it.

"Ball in their end zone."
4️⃣

2. Four downs to move 10 yards

Your team gets 4 "downs" (tries) to move the ball 10 yards forward. If you make it = fresh set of 4 downs. If you don't = other team gets the ball.

"4 tries. 10 yards."
🎽

3. Flags instead of tackles

In flag football, you wear a belt with two flags. To "tackle" someone, you pull off one of their flags. No grabbing jerseys, no shoving, no tackling.

"Pull the flag. Don't tackle."
🏈

4. Snap starts every play

The center starts the play by snapping the ball backward through their legs to the quarterback. Until the snap, no one can cross the line of scrimmage.

"No moving till the snap."
🎯

5. Pass or run

After the snap, the quarterback can: PASS the ball to a receiver, HAND IT OFF to a runner, or RUN with it. Pick the right play.

"Pass, hand off, or run."
🤲

6. Catching the pass

A pass is caught if BOTH feet are in bounds and you have the ball with control. Drop it, or step out = INCOMPLETE.

"Two feet. Two hands."
🚫

7. Sideline = out

Step on or over the sideline WITH the ball = the play is over. Ball comes back to where you stepped out.

"Step out = play over."
🏆

8. How scoring works

Touchdown (ball in end zone) = 6 points. Extra point (run/pass it in from 5 yards) = 1 point. (Field goals exist too, but most flag leagues skip them.)

"6 + 1 = 7."
🚩

9. Penalties (no's)

No tackling. No blocking with hands. No flag-guarding (covering your flags with your arms). No crossing the line before the snap. Penalty = the team that fouled loses yards.

"Play clean."
🤝

10. Be a great teammate

Pass to the open person. Cheer for your teammates. Listen to the coach. Shake hands after the game — win or lose. Football is a team sport, even in the backyard.

"Help. Cheer. Shake hands."

Every move in football has a name, a stance, and a path. You already know how to throw. Now let's name the moves and learn the secrets.

The 6 essential moves

🏈

Throwing (Spiral)

Grip the ball with fingers across the laces. Step toward your target. Rotate your hips and shoulders. Release as your arm comes over the top. Snap the wrist — that's what makes the SPIRAL.

"Laces. Step. Snap."
🤲

Catching

Two hands. Pinky fingers together for LOW passes (under your chest). Thumbs together for HIGH passes (above your chest). Look the ball INTO your hands. Then pull it tight.

"Look it in. Tuck it."
🏃

Running with the ball

Hold the ball TIGHT against your body with your strong arm. Cover both ends (one end in your hand, one in your armpit). Keep your other arm free for balance. Run hard.

"Tuck it tight. Run hard."
🎽

Pulling the flag

Get to the ball-carrier's hip area. Grab the FLAG (not the body). Pull straight away. Hold it up so the ref can see.

"Flag, not body."
🏃

Running routes

A "route" is the path a receiver runs after the snap. Slant: 3 steps forward, then cut at 45°. Out: straight then cut sideways. Post: straight then cut toward the goalpost. Plan it before the snap.

"Run the route I planned."
⬇️

The snap (center exchange)

The center holds the ball, bends over, and PUSHES it backward through their legs to the quarterback. Quarterback catches it and starts the play.

"Center to QB."

The Football Grip (for throwing)

Here's how to hold the ball when you throw:

  • Spread your fingers ACROSS the laces (the white stripes).
  • Index finger near the top, pinky near the bottom.
  • Thumb wraps UNDER the ball.
  • SMALL space between your palm and the ball (don't crush it).
"Fingers on laces. Thumb under."

The Receiver's Stance

Before every play, get ready:

  • Feet shoulder-width apart, one foot slightly back.
  • Knees bent, weight on the balls of your feet.
  • Hands ready at your chest, eyes UP looking at the QB.
  • Wait for the snap. THEN run your route.
"Ready. Watch. Go on the snap."

The Five Steps of Every Catch

1️⃣

See it

As soon as the ball leaves the QB's hand, find it in the air. Watch only the ball.

"Find it."
2️⃣

Move

Get your body in line with where the ball will land. Small fast steps.

"Get under it."
3️⃣

Hands

Two hands. Pinkies together for low passes; thumbs together for high passes.

"Make the diamond."
4️⃣

Catch

Look the ball INTO your hands. Soft hands — give a little as it arrives.

"Look it in."
5️⃣

Tuck & run

Pull the ball tight to your body, cover both ends, then RUN.

"Tuck. Then go."
The structure stays the same so Nicolas can predict what's coming. The content inside the structure changes so it never gets boring. Four lesson formats roll in over the year — one at a time, never all at once. Each format scales to a 60-min lesson with Coach or a 30-min practice with Daddy.

Why formats, not single-skill weeks

Nicolas needs predictable structure. He also needs novelty — but novelty inside the structure, not novelty AS the structure. So instead of "this week we do uppies, next week we do dribbles," every practice follows the same shape: warm up → play → new skill → play with skill → drill → clean up. The shape is the constant. What goes inside each block is what evolves.

Built-in transitions (water cooler, ball machine adjustments, lights on/off) are part of the practice, not interruptions. They reset attention between blocks. Plan for them.

The Four Formats

One format starts. A second is added at week 7. A third at week 27. A fourth at week 40. After each addition, formats rotate — never converge.

"Look at this. Let's try."

Weeks 1–6 (only format used) · Then in rotation through week 52

Foundation building. Lots of preview, lots of demo, lots of "let's see what this is." Skills are introduced gently and revisited often. Every concept Nicolas needs (court parts, grip, bounce-catch, balance, dribbles, uppies, ready position, drop-and-hit) lives inside this format — woven into the warm-up and the new-skill block instead of taking over the whole session.

The Warm-Up Menu

These are the foundation moves from the original "weeks 1–6." They never go away. They live in the warm-up of every format, every session — picking 1–3 each time, rotating through. New foundation moves get added; old ones fade and return.

🚶 Field Walk

Walk every line. Name each one out loud. Stand on the 50, the goal line, the end zone.

"Name every line."

🤸 Body Wake-Up

Jumping jacks, butt kicks, side shuffle, arm circles, high knees. 30 seconds each.

"Wake the body."

🏈 Ball Holds

Hold the ball with the proper grip. Fingers on laces. Switch hands. Build the "feel."

"Fingers on laces."

🤲 Soft Hands

Daddy gently tosses the ball at your chest. Catch with two hands, pinkies together for low, thumbs together for high. 10 in a row.

"Look it in."

🎯 Short Throws

5 yards apart. Throw spirals back and forth. Build distance only when the spiral is tight.

"Tight spirals first."

🏃 Route Walks

Walk through a route at slow speed. Slant. Out. Post. Get the cuts right before you sprint.

"Sharp cuts."

🎽 Flag Pull Drill

Daddy walks/jogs past you holding a flag. Pull it as he passes. Switch sides.

"Flag, not body."

⬇️ Center Snaps

Practice snapping the ball between your legs to a partner behind you. Smooth and accurate.

"Snap straight back."

🚦 Stop & Go

Sprint forward. On Daddy's whistle, STOP fast. On the next whistle, GO. Builds route control.

"Stop on the whistle."

🤾 Backpedal

Run BACKWARDS for 5 yards. Defenders need this to stay in front of receivers. Small fast steps, eyes UP.

"Eyes up. Small steps."

The 52-Week Rotation

Each week shows the format used and the new content focus inside it. Skills can rotate, fade, return, or be skipped. Life happens — there is no falling behind.

Phase 1 · Discover the Ball

Weeks 1–6 Format A only Same shape every time

Six weeks of the exact same lesson shape. Every block in the same order. The content inside the warm-up and the new-skill block rotates through the foundation moves so Nicolas gets familiar with all of them — but the structure never moves. This is when the format becomes predictable. After six weeks, he knows it.

Week 1A

Discover the Field

Warm-up: body wake-up + field walk. New skill: name every line (yard lines, end zones, goal posts). Play: tag in the end zone.

Week 2A

Discover the Ball

Warm-up: ball holds + body wake-up. New skill: proper grip (fingers on laces). Play: hand-off relay.

Week 3A

Discover the Catch

Warm-up: ball holds + soft hands. New skill: two-hand catch — thumbs together for high, pinkies together for low. Play: 10 chest catches in a row.

Week 4A

Discover the Throw

Warm-up: soft hands + short throws. New skill: the throwing motion — step, rotate, release with wrist snap. Play: 5-yard catch with Daddy.

Week 5A

Discover the Spiral

Warm-up: short throws. New skill: wrist snap that makes the ball SPIN. Tight spirals fly straighter.

Week 6A

Discover Running with the Ball

Warm-up: ball holds. New skill: tuck the ball tight, cover both ends, run with purpose.

Phase 2 · Adding Game Skills

Weeks 7–26 A & B alternating First format change

Format B joins. The two formats alternate every other week — A, then B, then A, then B. Format A keeps introducing new technical pieces (routes, throws, catches); Format B applies them in cooperative drills. Watch for Nicolas noticing the new format. Talk about it. Use the visual schedule to preview the difference.

Phase 3 · Adding Match Play

Weeks 27–39 A, B & C rotating Real scoring arrives

Format C enters. Now three formats rotate (A → B → C → A → B → C…). Format C is where real small-sided games happen. Format A keeps introducing new skills (reading defenses, stiff-arms, play calling). Format B keeps the cooperative drill muscle. Format C is where Nicolas practices the regulation challenge of competitive games with built-in safety: champion moments, water breaks, predictable structure.

Week 32B

Week 33C

Phase 4 · Becoming a Football Player

Weeks 40–52 A, B, C & D rotating Ownership emerges

Format D enters. All four rotate (A → B → C → D → A → B → C → D…). Format D is where Nicolas becomes the author. He picks his warm-up. He picks the skill to refine. He reflects at the end. The other formats keep doing what they do — but now there's a session every fourth week where he's running the show.

Drills get the body strong. Games get the spirit strong. Mix them in every practice. Most use one ball and an open yard — perfect for the backyard. Some need a buddy or two. Others are perfect for when Alexander joins.

500 CLASSIC

The backyard football classic. One player is the QUARTERBACK and throws the ball up. Everyone else tries to catch it. Catching = 100 points. Tipping it (then catching) = 75. Catching on one bounce = 50. First to 500 points becomes the new quarterback.

Why it's perfect for the brothers: built-in regulation breaks (each throw resets the field), social and silly, predictable structure with novelty, and Alexander can be the QB while Nicolas catches.

"Catch it. Add the points."
👋

Two-Hand Touch

Simpler than flag football — no flags needed. Two hands touch the ball-carrier below the shoulders = tackled. Otherwise same as flag.

"Two-hand tag."
🎯

Tire Throw (Target Practice)

Hang a tire from a tree (or use a hula hoop on a stand). Stand 10 yards away. Throw the football THROUGH the tire. 10 throws, count makes.

"Aim. Spiral. Through."
🏃

Hot Potato Football

Stand in a circle. Pass the ball as fast as possible. Drop it = you're out. Last one wins.

"Soft hands. Fast pass."
🚧

Cone Routes

Set up cones for slants, outs, posts. Run each route in turn. Daddy throws to the spot where the route ends.

"Sharp cuts. Look back."
🎽

Flag Tag

Wear flag belts. No ball. Just chase each other and pull flags. Last one with both flags wins.

"Flag, not body."
🚦

Red Light, Green Light Football

Daddy says "green!" — you sprint with the ball. "Red!" — STOP. "Yellow!" — slow motion. Builds ball control under pressure.

"Stop on red."
🏆

Touchdown Race

Daddy and Nicolas line up at the goal line. Daddy throws the ball downfield. Whoever catches it RUNS to the opposite goal line. Defender tries to pull the flag.

"Catch, then sprint."
🤲

Pop Pass Rally

Cooperative game. Quick short pops back and forth, 5 yards apart. How many in a row without dropping?

"Soft hands. Beat our record."
⏱️

Beat the Clock

60 seconds. How many "completions" (caught passes) can Daddy throw to Nicolas? Beat the score each week.

"Fast hands."
🥄

Pass & Spot

Each catch, you "spot" the ball where you caught it. Try to make it to a target (tree, end zone) in 5 catches.

"Move the ball forward."
🧱

Wall Spiral (Indoor)

Rainy day? Throw a small football against a wall, catch the rebound. Build the wrist snap.

"Snap the wrist."

Make any drill more fun

  • Add a story. "You're down by 4 with 10 seconds left. Throw the Hail Mary."
  • Add silly cues. "Aim for the tree. Hit the tree, win a million dollars."
  • Add a target. A cone, a tire, a chalk square. Aim is more fun than just throwing.
  • Add a count-out-loud. "Hut!... Hut!... HUT!" Builds rhythm. Builds confidence.
  • Add a victory move. Touchdown dance. Spike the ball (gently). The "first down" point.
  • Add music. Bring a speaker. Pick the songs. Football to a beat.

🏈 Local programs near Marion, MA

Backyard catch with Daddy is the right place to start. When Nicolas wants more, these are good local options — all flag football (no tackle for this age):

  • Old Rochester Youth Football — flag league for K–6 serving Marion, Mattapoisett, and Rochester. Fall season.
  • South Coast Flag Football — regional flag league with various age divisions.
  • NFL FLAG — the national NFL-sponsored youth flag program; has affiliated leagues in the SouthCoast region.
  • Belmont Hill Club — check for youth flag/touch football clinics, especially in the fall.
  • i9 Sports SouthCoast — kid-first flag football with an emphasis on fundamentals and fun.

Important: do not enroll in tackle football at this age. The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends flag through at least age 14 to protect developing brains and joints. Flag is the right move.

🇺🇸 NFL FLAG & USA Football

  • NFL FLAG — nflflag.com. The NFL's youth flag program. Skill videos, parent guides, league locator.
  • USA Football — usafootball.com. National governing body. The free "Football Development Model" coaching course is excellent — even for backyard Daddy-coaches.
  • Heads Up Football — USA Football's safety-first program. Worth reading even if you only play flag.

🏃 Integrating Sarah PT with football

Sarah's PT work pairs really well with football — it's organized sprinting, cutting, catching, and throwing.

  • Throwing mechanics. The spiral requires hip rotation, shoulder mobility, and core engagement firing in sequence. Sarah can evaluate Nicolas's throwing motion — small adjustments early prevent shoulder issues later.
  • Cutting and route running. Sharp cuts on slants and outs require knee tracking and decel control. Sarah's the expert. Apply her cueing during route work.
  • Catching mechanics. Soft hands, eyes on the ball, hand-eye coordination. Pair her catching reps with the "Soft Hands" warm-up drill.
  • Backpedal & defensive footwork. Hard on hips and ankles. Sarah can prescribe single-leg balance and ankle-strengthening work.
  • Breathing during effort. Football is anaerobic — short sprints, then rest between plays. Given the respiratory symptoms being investigated, this is actually a GOOD sport for Nicolas: built-in rest between plays. Pair Sarah's breath work with the between-play resets.
  • Share the warm-up menu. Print the 10 moves from this guide. Sarah can suggest which to emphasize and what to add.

Bonus framing: let Nicolas know that Sarah's PT work helps him throw further, cut sharper, and stay on the field longer.

🥾 Equipment for flag football

  • Football size. K2 (smallest) for ages 6–9. TDJ (junior) for 10–12. Adult size NOT until 13+. Wrong size makes throwing form impossible.
  • Mouthguard. Required by most flag leagues. Always boil-and-bite for fit. Replace yearly.
  • Cleats. Soccer cleats work for flag (rubber molded, not metal). Mid-cut gives ankle support.
  • Flag belt. League will provide or sell one. Get an extra for backyard practice — they break.
  • Receiver gloves (optional). Sticky-palmed gloves help with catching. Worth $15 once form is good.
  • Practice football at home. Always have a ball near the door.

🧠 Nicolas-Specific Suggestions

  • Pre-practice routine. Same words, same order. "Mouthguard. Cleats. Water. Ball. Backyard."
  • Built-in rest is a feature. Football has natural pauses between plays. Use them. Given the respiratory symptoms being investigated, this is one of the better sport choices — breathe between every snap.
  • Breathing awareness during sprints. Pair Sarah's breath work with route running. Exhale long on the sprint.
  • Pre-practice snack. 60 min after eating works best. Familiar safe foods.
  • Visual schedule on the practice bag. Same as the other sports.
  • End every practice on a catch. Don't let the last throw be a drop.
  • Film the wins. Quick clips from the backyard.
  • Watch a Patriots game together. A great way to introduce real football. Pause to explain things.
Football is not just a sport. It's a 45-minute practice in self-regulation, motor planning, hand-eye coordination, communication, teamwork, and joyful effort. The American backyard classic — every front lawn, every fall afternoon. Every practice is a developmental gift.
